  narration: "<p>NopeCopter:</p><p></p><p>Being a civ with high expectations is a tough position to be in. If you do well, you’re boring. If you don’t, you’re a disappointment. There’s no winning. Such was the dilemma faced by the Guaycuru going into CBRX5. Ranked 7th on the initial Power Rankings, the Guaycuru had already picked up a reputation for strength in previous AI games, and while their test game record didn’t entirely reflect this (mainly because the Chono were also strong), they certainly weren’t floundering, either. Some had already begun to bemoan their presence in the game, absolutely certain that they’d ruin the balance of things. There simply wasn’t a universe where the Guaycuru would turn out to be a fan favorite.</p><p></p><p>The Guaycuru opened up the game with a textbook flashy play, one that the Power Rankers all knew and dreaded: an invasion of the Xavante. In the test games, this had nearly always led to the swift elimination of their northern neighbor, especially with the support of the Potiguara. However, something was different this time. The Guaycuru had gotten off to a slow start, and the Xavante cities were positioned in just the right places to confuse their enemy forces, leading to a complete failure of an invasion. All the while, Caral, Potiguara, and the Chono just kept on settling, and before the Guaycuru could get the ball rolling, they were surrounded. Like so many would-be top powers of the season, they had fumbled in dramatic fashion. By Episode 4, they had plummeted all the way from 7th place down to 49th. </p><p></p><p>However, there’s always room for a resourceful civ to claw back a bit of relevance in the early game, even if they need to resort to… unscrupulous methods. For the Guaycuru, this meant taking advantage of their neighbors’ rush to forward-settle them. Sure, they no longer had any city spots available in their IMMEDIATE vicinity… but there were still a few empty regions in Patagonia that the Chono had neglected to fill out. And so, Laguna Martin was settled directly in the middle of the Chono core. They followed this up by claiming the Falklands and the very tip of Tierra del Fuego, completely disrupting the Chono’s ability to move troops around their empire. This would, in any normal circumstance, have gotten them killed for infringing on another civ’s territory so flagrantly. However, the Guaycuru gambit paid off, because it turned out that the Chono just did not care to play the game at all. The Guaycuru had just about doubled their city count, and all it cost was any shreds of respect the fans still had for them.</p><p></p><p>Of course, just because they miraculously avoided getting mauled by a seal for their settling habits, didn’t mean they were actually all that much better-looking. Their PR rank improved slightly (though even the Power Rankers were certain a war with the Chono was coming), but they couldn’t move units through these cities very well, and so they lacked an actual power base in a region full of strong civs. The Guaycuru went multiple episodes without a single mention, just sort of floating through the game, waiting for someone stronger to build up enough firepower to put them out of their misery.</p><p></p><p>That was, at least, until the Guaycuru decided to actually do something. Suddenly, after going around 5 episodes with literally one mention total, they sprung into action and declared war on the Potiguara. Despite being nominally weaker, they had managed to isolate the cheeky Potiguara forward-settle of Cachoeira in IRL Uruguay, and the city fell with little resistance. It was a bit of a freebie, for sure, but it was also one of the first things that had actually happened in South America all game. That wasn’t enough for them, however - the Guaycuru then proceeded to continue pushing north, taking the city of Catu! Despite everything, it turned out that the Guaycuru weren’t just willing to sit on their hands and wait for their turn to die like some other failed top-tiers. They were the only civ in South America that actually gave a damn, and they were here to win.</p><p></p><p>The Guaycuru attempted to continue their winning streak with a very bold declaration of war against the Xavante, but since they’d just given up a city to Caral (for no real reason, mind you), this was a dud. That actually worked in the Guaycuru’s favor, however, as the Xavante had a much larger military presence on their border and would have certainly won. Their reputation had genuinely somewhat recovered by this point, and they were considered a solid mid-tier - 27th in the Episode 16 Power Rankings. That was higher than the Potiguara!</p><p></p><p>Unfortunately, however, that ranking may have been a bit premature. The Guaycuru, getting hooked on their first taste of success, fell victim to a tragic flaw: hubris. They declared war on the Potiguara once again, expecting to make further gains, but this time their opponents were prepared. Lacking proper military build-up and unable to use many of their units (due to them being trapped behind Chono borders), the Potiguara quickly demolished the Guaycuru offensive and began to surround Catu. And then… they… didn’t damage it. They just kind of… sat there. For several straight episodes, despite their nominal overwhelming advantage, they just completely ignored Catu. They even tried moving past it, as if they’d already taken the city! The second Guaycuru-Potiguara War began in Episode 17, and it took until Episode 23 for Catu to finally fall despite the near-total lack of resistance. The Guaycuru, for their part, did have the tech advantage for this entire period, but they simply lacked the Production and organizational know-how to launch any sort of counter-offensive or even break the siege. When the Potiguara unlocked Musketmen and finally got their act together, it was over. Guaycuru cities began falling like dominoes, until suddenly the Potiguara were at the gates of Cachoeira once more. Funnily enough, they did let the Guaycuru keep the city, perhaps as a symbol of their defeat and humiliation, but by this point it was over. The Guaycuru had lost everything, including their capital, and the Potiguara were once again ascendant.</p><p></p><p>At least, that’s how it was supposed to go. For somehow, despite everything, the Guaycuru once again found a way to claw at fate and spite their enemies. The Potiguara could have their cities, sure… but they would ONLY get their cities. Using a truly ridiculous number of Citadels, the Guaycuru managed to reclaim nearly every tile they had lost in their prior war, rendering the Potiguara gains all but pointless. But this wasn’t even their biggest play. It turned out that they were not the only civ that had recently been devastated by a war. The Xaragua, in the Caribbean, had recently been completely wiped out by the Yanomami… except for one lone Antarctic colony. A colony that only the Guaycuru had access to. And of course, the Guaycuru could hardly pass up the opportunity for an elimination on their record after all their bad luck. And so, they declared war, and delivered the killing blow to one of the only civs less fortunate than themselves, perhaps restoring some small fraction of their pride in the process.</p><p></p><p>However, once again the Guaycuru’s hubris came back to bite them, because it seems that this kill was what finally pushed their luck over the edge. The Chono awoke from their slumber and finally, finally turned their guns on the civ that had been passively sabotaging them for the entire game. By this point, even ignoring the massive size and position advantage the Chono held, the mere fact that the Chono had planes was enough to seal the fate of the Guaycuru. They were wiped off the map with a level of overkill rarely seen in the CBR, and much like the Xaragua, even their Antarctic colonies couldn’t save them.</p><p></p><p>The Guaycuru were… a controversial civ, to say the least. Many despised them for their bordergore, for their disappointing opening, and for their supposed “slowing down of the game” by not dying to the Chono earlier. They certainly weren’t what was promised. And yet, they were also scrappy, and they refused to lay down and die. They made actual meaningful progress in South America at a time where none of the supposed top-tiers could manage the same. Sure, all their failures were self-inflicted, but that’s what made them so fun, and they always found a way to try and recover from their mistakes. The Guaycuru were a tragic figure in the most classic sense, a hero with a fatal flaw that proved to be their undoing, and they played the part perfectly. There was no universe where the Guaycuru would turn out to be a fan favorite, but this is probably about as close as they could have reasonably gotten. F.</p>"

Spearthrower Owl took advantage of the new 2 tile coastal settlement rule to settle more cities in Mesoamerica than any other civ in the history of the CBRX. Teotihuacan was truly a wide empire, spreading themselves from the mountains of Arizona down to the Panama Canal itself. They even managed to stretch their borders into the Pacific at the dawn of the exploration age, sending settlers into Polynesia to settle lands that Rapa Nui had failed to claim. Though they missed out of the Hawaiian islands that their northern neighbour Pomo were able to grab, Teotihuacan was truly the empire both on land and at sea.</p><p></p><p>That spreading would be perhaps more trouble than it was worth. This is a civ with a 10 bias in wonder building mind you, just about as high as you can get in the game. But with a lacking science base in the beginning of the game, and some happiness and gold troubles from settling so many cities early, Teotihuacan was never at the forefront of technology enough to be able to compete with other heavy wonder builders in the game, namely Tlingit, Tang, and Xaragua of all civs.</p><p></p><p>Speaking of Xaragua, Teotihuacan was not without wars in the early game. Teotihuacan also settled the Panama Canal, being the first civ in this game to be able to traverse easily from the Pacific to the Atlantic. Unfortunately, the Panama Canal settlement of Cholula was a bit too far away from the core of their empire, and fell in a war with the Yanomami. Other conflicts broke out with neighbours throughout the game, scattered assaults between the Ponca, Susquehannock, and Caral. Though their successes at first were hampered by rough terrain and strategic indecisiveness, losing out a Baja California city capture to none other but their allies the Cebu, Teotihuacan managed in their second coalition to capture two of Pomo’s cities, ultimately spelling the extremely early demise of Pomo from this game.</p><p></p><p>That demise however was their undoing, as by allying themselves with Tlingit, they inadvertently put themselves right next to Tlingit’s borders, and accrued enough diplomatic malus from conquering Pomo that Tlingit would decide to continue their march southward. Teotihuacan fought bitterly against their once allies, despite both a technological and a production disparity. The science and happiness problems of the early game came to bite Teotihuacan in the back, as their crossbows and musketmen fought against field guns and cavalry. Teotihuacan managed to hold the tide for a full two episodes, flipping their border cities back and forth in a desperate push, until finally Tlingit managed to research flight, and bomb Teotihuacan’s walls from the sky in a blitzkrieg of an attack. With naval superiority swooping around the Cebu city in Baja, and Caral pestering them in the south, Teotihuacan crumbled under the pressure, until the armies were marching at the doors of the capital. Only a desperate peace deal with Tlingit, gifting away three cities including the former Karankawa Cujane, could stem the tide of Tlingit’s might and superior firepower.</p><p></p><p>From there, Teotihuacan continued to decline. There was a small spark of hope as Teotihuacan managed an assault on Galapagos Caral, but ultimately Spearthrower Owl’s Polynesian holdings began falling one by one. A coalition war declared by Susquehannock and Chono sealed the deal, as Teotihuacan Polynesia was quickly wiped out, and the remnants of their mesoamerican empire was picked away by destroyers and bombers. Even Tlingit could not stand idly by, pushing their borders towards the Teotihuacan capital of Teotihuacan with audacious citadels eating all of the land around the city. Though Chono peaced out before the final city of Teotihuacan could be captured, a well placed Susquehannock Landship was able to sneak through twisted and convoluted borders, and the city fell in one last gasp of resignation. Despite contested borders, poor science prospects, constant happiness issues, and an ally turned enemy in a dramatic fashion, Teotihuacan fought until the very bitter end, in a way that I think exceeded everyone's expectations.
