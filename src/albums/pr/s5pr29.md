And picking up their ancestor worship pantheon decently quickly, Ethiopia looked to be leveraging their cultural output as well. </p><p></p><p>As a civilization with abilities focused around culture and religion, it's fair to say that Ethiopia wasn’t perfectly suited for a domination focused game like this. Despite their very solid initial positioning, Ethiopia’s unique abilities were focused on taking faith and great writer points and turning them into religious advantages. Though their ability to take generated faith and turn it into city defense and health was interesting, ultimately Ethiopia was focused on cultural victories instead of military victories. And though their abilities leveraged faith well, their neighbour Hyksos to the north got an incredibly fast religion, sweeping Pesedjet through Africa before Ethiopia could even consider picking up a religion of their own. Ethiopia became locked out of a religious game, beholden to their holy northern neighbours for a lot of their Unique Advantages.</p><p></p><p>Despite this focus on religion and culture, Ethiopia was able to leverage their productive starting location well, building one of the largest armies of the ancient and classical era. With many neighbours to their north, east, south and west, Ethiopia decided to turn their ire towards Zazzau, a decision that would start a thousands of years feud that would cause cities to trade hands and armies to spill countless amounts of blood on the battlefield. In episode 7, Zara would build the Statue of Zeus in the city of Roha, arguably Ethiopia’s best city. Situated in productive hills, on the shores of fertile Lake Victoria, and protected by rough terrain and mountains, grabbing arguably the best military focused wonder in the game was an incredible boon for Ethiopia, spelling out a clear line of success in this game. Ethiopia would then leverage this boon to invade Maravi, taking a city purely with the power of horseman and that additional +15% combat strength against cities. They would try again to get another Maravi city to the south, but would be beaten out by Luba, blocking them off from any advances further south.</p><p></p><p>It’s fair to say that Ethiopia was blocked for a lot of this game, with Luba to the north being nearly unassailable by most civs, Seychelles to the east blocking off any ocean access, Zazzau to the west as a perpetual enemy, and Hyksos to the north as the seat of religious power in the region. Ethiopia, if they had a more military focused inclination to their actions, should have invaded Hyksos early in this game, as the lands of Egypt are flat and easy to maneuver through, and early in this game Hyksos was quite weak in terms of military strength and productive capability. If Ethiopia could have marched northward, claimed Hyksos’ land and seat of holy power, and wiped them from the game, there’s a solid chance that Zara Yaqob could have leveraged the diplomatic boons from holding Africa’s main religion to fight holy wars across the continent. But, Ethiopia instead continued to hold animosity towards Zazzau, much to their detriment.</p><p></p><p>Many conflicts with Zazzau continued to erupt throughout this game, as Amina pushed first with horsemen, then with their unique knight replacement, and then with lancers and cavalry. And though Zazzau would often take cities, Ethiopia would often take them right back, trading losses in Ethiopia’s hard to defend, flatland extents of their western empire. These wars likely could have gone in Ethiopia’s favour, if not for Seychelles to the east joining Zazzau in coalition, splitting and weakening Ethiopia's forces. Though losses on the eastern front would be much less, due to mountainous terrain and defendable cities, this diverting of attention would ultimately be the cause of Ethiopia’s downfall.</p><p></p><p>Despite a period of respite, where both Zazzau and Seychelles peaced out with Ethiopia, their prospects continued to look worrying. Zara would decide in Episode 18 to attack Hyksos, but too little too late their invasion would yield no gains. Hyksos, once unproductive and lacking in technological advancement, would have the chance to build up a well stocked and well advanced army, enough to repeal Ethiopia's forces in an evenly sided battle. And, with Ethiopia’s armies depleted in the north, Zazzau and Seychelles would once again conspire together to chew away at Ethiopia’s other holdings.</p><p></p><p>As episode and episode would drag on, the borders to the east would ebb and flow, slowly but surely tightening Ethiopia’s extent more towards their mountainous core. And, as Ethiopia’s science dwindled, Seychelle’s research output would flourish, letting them field advanced line infantry and field guns on the western front. In a desperate bid to ease the two front attacks, Ethiopia would ultimately peace out with Seychelles, gifting them Harar along the Nile and Roha along Lake Victoria to smooth over the deal. Losing out on two of their key cities, and no longer holding onto the defensive wall and Statue of Zeus city of Roha, Ethiopia would dwindle down to two cities holding against the tide of Zazzau’s military complex.</p><p></p><p>In this weakened state, long time ally Luba would finally take their chance to strike, conquering Addis Ababa and leaving Ethiopia with only their capital remaining. Though the approach to the city through a one tile wide gap would ultimately be unassailable, the continued pressure that Luba would put on Ethiopia was oppressive and dwindled their army to nothing. It left Zara in such a precarious position, that when Seychelles ultimately rejoined the war, this time with Artillery and Great War Bombers, they were able to drop the capital’s health down to 0 in a single turn. The Statue of Zeus, once a key part of Ethiopia’s long term domination plan, would be their downfall as a single Seychelles cavalry swept through the mountain passes and eliminated Ethiopia from this game.</p><p></p><p>Ultimately, Ethiopia’s story is one of missed potential. With a solid starting position, a key wonder pickup, and a large and advanced military to boot, Ethiopia could have been one of the most powerful contenders in Africa. But, picking far away targets with difficult to navigate geography over easy, close by targets with religions significance bit Zara Yaqob in the behind. Missing out on naval settlements along the Red Sea and the Horn of Africa kept them safe from naval assaults, but kept them landlocked in a region with defensible neighbours, stopping Ethiopia from pushing out and gaining real space past their initial extent of early game settling. If Zazzau had been just a bit weaker, or Hyksos had been just a bit more tantalizing of a target, or Seychelles had focused just a bit more to their own eastern extent, Ethiopia could have made a real push in this game. But, that's how things shake out sometimes in a battle royale. Like other early game powerhouses, Pomo and Teotihuacan in North America, or Lanfang in South East Asia, Ethiopia was overestimated and ultimately lost out to less assuming neighbours that were able to slowly but surely wear them down.</p>"
