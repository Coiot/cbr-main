The term originates from X2, where Karl XII’s Sweden (who had gained a reputation for being an incredibly strong AI) ended up completely fumbling the game. Since Gustavus Adolphus of Mk. 2 fame was the Lion of the North, Karl must therefore have been the Catboy of the North. Since then, we’ve had plenty of underperformers, and each season has had its own “catboy” (the Seneca stand out as X4’s top pick), and X5 certainly has had its fair share of disappointments. But for my money, the Catboy of X5 is definitely the Ponca.</p><p></p><p>Ponca’s really strong starting position (despite what some might say, the Great Plains are a great place to start generally because there’s just so much food and a lot of easy expansion), strong uniques, and expansive AI made them an absolute favorite going into X5, and personally, I couldn’t have been happier about that. Not just because I helped make the civ, mind you - though that did help - but because the art, the colors, the historical context, the possibility for a very funny settlement in someone else’s lands, it was all a slam dunk. The Ponca were ranked 6th place in the Episode 0 Power Rankings, and it seemed like they were quite popular, to boot.</p><p></p><p>So, naturally, the very first thing the Ponca did was ignore all their open space and Farm-based Uniques to settle their second city on a tile surrounded by four Mountains.</p><p></p><p>This second city would define the rest of the Ponca’s game, mostly for the worse. The lack of Food and Production meant they were slow to build up a carpet and slower to build Settlers, especially given how expansive the Pomo and Anishinaabe were being. The Pomo even launched an early invasion (which, to the Ponca’s credit, was entirely pointless in the face of the comically defensive city of Wain-Xude), further starving out Ponca’s already limited resources. By the time they kinda sorta woke up, the Pomo, Anishinaabe, and Karankawa were sniping their last real available city locations right as they managed to move their Settler into position (aside from one spot they just patently refused to settle). Ponca’s PR rank, naturally, collapsed in the process - it dropped from 6th to 22nd after the first episode, then to 42nd by episode 3, and by Episode 5 they were placed 49th.</p><p></p><p>Surprisingly, once Ponca was confined to a tiny little area with only three cities to their name, they kind of stopped being relevant. In fact, they decided that they were just going to be really, really nice to everybody. As North America was engulfed in war, Ponca alone mostly avoided the fighting. Instead of joining the giant anti-Anishinaabe coalition, the Ponca declared friendship instead. Despite their sweetness, however, the Karankawa still saw fit to declare war and pick on them, almost taking Hubdon in the process… only for them to suddenly be overwhelmed with guilt and sue for peace, giving up a city in the process. And that’s the story of how the Ponca got a fourth city.</p><p></p><p>The Ponca did try a counter-attack on the Karankawa a couple of episodes later as they entered their “””villain arc”””, mirroring the anti-Pomo coalition, but naturally they sucked and failed to take anything. As they were fumbling an easy war, however, the Ponca were suddenly attacked by another superpower: the Anishinaabe! Except, of course, that the Anishinaabe secretly also kind of sucked, and their invasion took a while to get going. Eventually they did capture Nanza (even though Ponca also managed to settle another city finally), and the Susquehannock also scored a city, but it took quite some time thanks to a shockingly valiant Ponca resistance effort. Still, Ponca was facing down a coalition of two major powers, and their fate was all but sealed… except, of course, it wasn’t actually, because of their big stupid mountain city. The Anishinaabe let the Ponca off the hook with two cities left to their name, and while the Ponca were now well and truly out of the game, they at least had a good view for what would come next.</p><p></p><p>The fact that random rump states will outlive serious contenders who just happen to get unlucky is as inevitable as it is frustrating in the CBR. However, looking at it another way, it can also be very, very cathartic from the perspective of those rump states who got screwed over (in part) by those major powers. The Ponca failed mostly because of their own ineptitude, but the Pomo certainly didn’t help. But, for all the city spots they nabbed from the Ponca while the latter was struggling to get off the ground, the one thing they DIDN’T settle was a reasonably defensible mountain city. And so, as the once-mighty Pomo disintegrated in the face of Teotihuacan, the Ponca watched from their front-row seat, laughing. The Karankawa, too, were smacked around real well by the Susquehannock, which must have been quite satisfying. The Ponca even managed to pick up Musketmen, not that they helped when the Anishinaabe returned to take Hubdon. But they didn’t get Wain-Xude. Wain-Xude was impenetrable.</p><p></p><p> …Well, it was impenetrable until aircraft were unlocked, at least. And to be fair, the city did buy them an extra five or so episodes after being reduced to a city-state. But eventually, the Anishinaabe returned for round 3 and knocked them out unceremoniously at the end of Episode 30. So it goes.</p><p></p><p>So, did the Ponca have a run to be proud of? At first glance, it might not seem like it - they fumbled the opening, got their lunch stolen, and did nothing as they slowly got chipped away. But if you really think about it… <br><br>…<br><br>…Nah, I’ve got nothing. This was a pretty pathetic showing, and it can’t even be blamed on another civ’s interference like, say, Ryukyu or Wassoulou or even Maravi. Sure, the Ponca had their settling spots taken, but they barely built any Settlers anyways. They did terribly and fumbled the only war they ever declared, only ever gaining a single city effectively out of pity. At most, you can say that Wain-Xude was a genius play defensively that left them survive much longer than they otherwise would have, but for a civ with as much promise as the Ponca, that’s a pathetic measure of success. If nothing else, they get points for outliving the Pomo, even though they absolutely didn’t deserve it - that is still kind of funny. But man… imagine what could have been. RIP (Rest In Ponk), may you get your revenge one of these days alongside the likes of the Comanche and Shawnee.</p>"
