But did he manage to make good use of this blessing? Well… it’s complicated, I’d say.</p><p></p><p>The Namib and Kalahari Deserts don’t exactly make for the best starting position, but the Herero were able to put up a decent showing in the X5 test runs, leading to them being initially ranked around the middle of the pack at 28th. The region was generally considered a toss-up, but not one that particularly favored the Herero. Going into the first couple of episodes, things didn’t seem all that promising - the Maravi and Luba settled much faster than them, and they seemed like they were destined to be crowded out. But the Herero weren’t just about to sit idly by. Instead, they took the other, much riskier route to early-game success: war! They launched an early invasion of Luba, committing a sizable (for the time) force to take on Luba’s capital, and… naturally, it went nowhere. This should have been the end for the Herero, really, being behind on cities and now also low on units, but thankfully for them, the Maravi had been settling in a truly baffling straight line. This gave the Herero enough room to keep growing and even choke out the Maravi somewhat, crippling their most problematic neighbor. The Herero were able to recover and grow to a respectable size, and before long, they decided the time was right to get their revenge on Luba and redeem themselves in the eyes of the fans. Naturally, this invasion attempt was even less successful than the last, and it cemented to us all that the Herero were not, in fact, going to be a major power. Much like his fellow returning leader Jandamarra, Morenga simply didn’t have the military skill to win a war.</p><p></p><p>Realizing that Luba was a no-go, the Herero then turned their attention toward their other neighbor, the Maravi. Together with the Seychelles, the plucky island civ which surely wasn’t a threat to a reasonably put-together mainlander, they launched an invasion of the boxed-out Maravi, and while the war got off to a poor start, they eventually turned it around and managed to secure the city of Maano. That was also the only city they managed to capture. Even though the Maravi had several undefended cities and were under attack from all directions, the Herero just couldn’t push any further. It was a pretty sad result from a civ that still seemed to maybe have a bit of promise, and so the Herero decided to try and cheer themselves up by invading Luba again! Naturally, the fighting quickly stalled and peace was made in the same episode the war began, but I guess they tried, at the very least.</p><p></p><p>Things died down for a couple of episodes after that, until the Herero, tired of their constant military failures, devised a bold new plan: what if they attacked Luba… AND the Maravi… at the SAME TIME? It was just crazy enough to work, especially since their military was much larger than either of the other civs’. Unfortunately, their military was also almost entirely made up of mounted units which struggled to damage cities. Luba once again proved too tough to break (somehow), but the Herero did manage to take one more city off the Maravi. Heck, they almost took a second one before they chickened out and accepted a pointless peace deal, too! To celebrate this victory, they launched a FIFTH invasion of Luba, and while this one - you guessed it - immediately stalemated and resulted in peace only a few turns later, everybody was surely in very high spirits throughout. </p><p></p><p>After this, the Herero took a very well-deserved break for half a dozen episodes or so, before finally realizing that they could totally just invade the Maravi again. Their relatively small navy made things a bit difficult at first, but their far superior tech meant that victory was inevitable if they just kept at it, and for once they actually did commit properly. The Maravi were reduced to a single city, finally giving the Herero the proper victory they’d been craving, although the fact that they razed a sizable portion of their gains did put a damper on things. Any celebration was short-lived, though, because now it was time for the Luba to be the ones invading the Herero, and their tech was even better than the Herero’s. The fighting was bloody, and the Herero city of Okakarara eventually fell to the pressure of the advanced Luba forces, but for the most part they managed to hold the line until peace was once again secured. The Herero had made it out mostly intact, but now it was more clear than ever that they were never going to break out… especially because the Seychelles had turned out to not be such a non-issue after all.</p><p></p><p>The Herero did attempt to invade the Maravi one last time and finish them off, but the terrain proved simply too difficult to maneuver, and not long afterward Luba got the done themselves. Now the Herero were truly trapped, with no further easy avenues of expansion. They did what they could to catch up to Luba in terms of tech, but given how their previous wars had gone, even that didn’t seem like a very promising direction. Rather than try for a seventh war with their old rivals (which they honestly might have been able to win), they merely sat around as the Seychelles, who had already wiped out Ethiopia, crushed Luba themselves and reduced them to a small rump state. Perhaps this could have been the Herero’s ticket out of their terrible position, but unfortunately, it was not to be. The Seychelles weren’t satisfied. Immediately after dealing with Luba, they set their sights on the Herero, and they were ruthless. The Herero did put up an admirable fight, punishing the Seychelles’ greed as best they could with an early offensive into the newly-captured Luba cities, but ultimately they just lacked the tech and the production to keep it going. The war devolved into a bit of a stalemate, with the Herero constantly flipping back cities and making the Seychelles bleed for every inch. But the Herero had a weak link: their leader. Eventually, Moringa cracked, and the Herero surrendered a massive chunk of their empire for peace, removing the last bits of hope for any sort of comeback. Before long, the Seychelles returned for a second round, and this time the Herero were wiped off the map without any sort of meaningful resistance, finally putting their time on the cylinder to an end.</p><p></p><p>So, did Jacob Morenga redeem himself? Well, he certainly did better than last time. The Herero were never a major power, but they were a solid mid-tier for almost their entire run, and while they did suck at war, they managed to pull off a couple of victories. That being said, they never got to really look cool, either. They kept getting stuck in stalemates or underperforming against opponents they really should have beaten, and in the end, even their heroic last stand was undercut by an awful peace deal. For a first-timer, this would be a perfectly respectable performance. For a second-timer, though, there’s definitely a feeling of disappointment, a lack of fulfillment in what could have been a pretty neat story if they’d just managed to get a bit further. I suppose it took Nzinga until her third attempt to be a major power, so maybe one day Morenga will return again and finally take Africa by the horns. But I kind of doubt it, honestly. If this is the best Morenga can do with a floundering eastern neighbor and a solid production base, he might just not have it in him. Regardless, he put up a decent fight, and he’s got that “Most Improved” award in the bag given how the Bunuba did. F.</p>"
