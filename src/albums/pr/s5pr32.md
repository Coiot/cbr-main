The difference in land quality alone is often just too much for the western/northern civ to make up for, let alone things like the difference in neighbors. The Anangu never stood a chance, the Yolngu never stood a chance, and while the Noongar put up a good fight, they were trapped in a stalemate where they were (on paper) the weaker party for the entire game. So… why not give them another go?</p><p></p><p>The Bunuba were honestly not expected to do all that well going into testing, with a bit of a reputation as a non-expansive and unambitious civ. Thankfully, this idea was proven… mostly wrong in the test runs. With the X5 modset, the Bunuba were consistently getting into solid positions and building up solid-looking empires. There was just one problem: their neighbor. While the Bunuba consistently did WELL, New South Wales consistently did EXCELLENTLY, nearly always building up a top empire and even at their worst still looking even with the Bunuba. There was not a single test game where the Bunuba were the dominant Australian civ, at least not by any significant margin. Not a great sign going into the full game, but anything could happen, and the Bunuba at least seemed capable of building a solid foundation to work off of.</p><p></p><p>Things seemed to be looking up right out of the gate as the Bunuba took an early lead, settling a strong core (though they notably ignored Uluru) and even securing the Great Library. Unfortunately, while New South Wales had a bit of a slow start, this turned out to be a temporary issue, as they quickly picked up steam and became a serious rival. Given the outcomes of Australias past, this would seem like a terrible, terrible situation… but the Bunuba had been here before, and they were ready this time. They had just unlocked Swordsmen, their military was one of the strongest on the cylinder, and the New South Welsh frontier was undefended. The Bunuba were off to war, and everything looked perfect. The Bunuba rocketed up to second place in the Power Rankings, and it seemed the curse of Western Australia would finally be broken.</p><p></p><p>It was in this war that we learned the truth of the Bunuba, however. They had a fatal flaw. And that flaw… was being absolutely hopeless at war. Their seemingly perfect invasion plan disintegrated on impact, resulting in a return to the status quo. On top of that, New South Wales settled the fertile southwest of the continent, locking the Bunuba out of some valuable land. Before long, New South Wales had overtaken the Bunuba positionally and even taken out a Rapa Nui colony. The Bunuba tried to follow suit, attacking the Papuan settlement of Hanga Piko… but, well, it was the Bunuba doing the invading. They barely even got a chance to scratch the city before it was handed over to Caral.</p><p></p><p>Still, things were far from over for the Bunuba. Their Science output was excellent, they had a solid core, and they even secured another valuable Wonder, one which would come to define them going forward: the Great Wall. And what great timing they had! Right after constructing the Great Wall, New South Wales launched an invasion which, on land at least, was thwarted without much effort. However, the Bunuba’s attempt at a counter-attack fizzled out, and thanks to their sizable navy in the southwest, the New South Welsh still managed to take a city in the war. At the very least the Bunuba had managed to settle in Indonesia, but at this point confidence in their abilities was dropping rapidly.</p><p></p><p>After this defeat, the Bunuba began to look elsewhere, building more Science wonders, securing Uluru (finally), and then declaring war on Ternate. Surely they could trounce one of the weakest civs on the cylinder… right? Well, at first, it seemed like they absolutely could! They captured Dodinga without much issue, then Galela… and then New South Wales and Lanfang declared war. All of their Indonesian colonies were gone in an instant, Lanfang ships were knocking on the coastline, and then to top it all off, Ternate completely reversed all of their losses in the war, forcing the Bunuba to accept status quo ante bellum. With Ternate. At the very least they managed to avoid losing any more cities to New South Wales, which was nice. And hey! They were at war with the Chono as well, and the Chono had an undefended Papuan city they could easily grab. They even started rebounding from their losses in Southeast Asia by colonizing Polynesia! And then they, uh… made peace with the Chono without taking any cities. Giving up their new Polynesian city in the process. </p><p></p><p>By this point it was clear the Bunuba had no shot at victory. They had solid stats but zero sauce to back it up. Their tech was still top-of-the-line, but they kept using that to unlock units that would not help them in the slightest. Hell, they even lost Uluru to a New South Welsh Citadel, as if everything else wasn’t bad enough. But at the very least the Bunuba were stubborn. They eventually managed to retake Dodinga by invading Caral, who had picked up the city in a peace deal with Ternate. They got back to settling Polynesia, too! Unfortunately, grit can only get you so far when you suck at life and everybody wants you dead. New South Wales and the Chono declared ANOTHER joint war, and with New South Wales packing Carracks, things looked rough. But then they finally took the Chono Papuan city of Quellon! And they managed to go on the offensive in the Outback, threatening Sorrell! …And then they made peace with New South Wales despite having the advantage. At the very least they got to keep their Polynesian colony this time, allowing them to further expand into the last great unclaimed region of the cylinder.</p><p></p><p>From here, the Bunuba stepped up their naval game with Carracks and Galleasses, and used the opportunity to get revenge on Lanfang, narrowly managing to retake the lost city of Gurang Ngadja on Bali. The good times didn’t last however, as within a few turns another anti-Bunuba coalition had formed. The Chono and Caral dismantled much of the Bunuba Polynesian holdings, and the once-mighty Bunuba Swordsmen were now forced to try and fend off New South Welsh Musketmen. They… honestly did surprisingly well at this, though they still lost a city. Once again, the Bunuba managed to avoid total devastation, but they were still being slowly chipped away, decade by decade. They sure as hell weren’t winning, only losing very very slowly. </p><p></p><p>The Bunuba tried one more time to regain some lost honor by at least eliminating Ternate, but even there they failed, being beaten to the punch by the Chono. And from there, the Bunuba had effectively run out of things to do. Their tech wasn’t awful by any means, but all of their neighbors were more than tough enough to keep pace, and for a civ like the Bunuba, that was far too much of a hurdle. Worse yet, though, was that New South Wales was learning. They had realized, through countless stalemated wars, that conquering the Bunuba was not going to work. And so, they had started to employ a different strategy: just… going around them. They attacked Mysore, they attacked the Seychelles, and most importantly, they attacked Lanfang. Their planes devastated the unprepared Lanfang fleet and secured practically all of Indonesia. This not only gave them even more expansion opportunities, but also boxed the Bunuba in even harder. Naturally, the Bunuba did not take the opportunity to stab New South Wales in the back during any of this. In fact, things were actually even worse for them than it appeared at first glance - their economy completely collapsed, rendering their once-incredible Science output completely moot and leaving them solidly in New South Wales’s dust.</p><p></p><p>And so, when New South Wales finally came back for one last dance (with fellow top-tier Tlingit in tow), even the surprisingly meaty Bunuba carpet couldn’t put up much of a fight. Their units were all bombed into oblivion, a Citadel chain provided an easy path into their capital, and the Tlingit quickly picked up all their Polynesian Islands. A civ that was, by all accounts, still a mid-tier player (albeit one without a single shot at victory) was wiped off the map in a grand total of nine turns. It was an ignoble end for a civ that really did deserve better, but at least they weren’t left to suffer as a rump state.</p><p></p><p>So… is the Western Australian civ truly always doomed? Honestly, I don’t believe so. Had the Bunuba not bordered one of the single strongest Australian civs in history, they very well could have gone further. Unfortunately, though, that’s not the world we live in. The Bunuba, both through their own incompetence and their uncanny ability to invite coalition wars against themselves, had their fate sealed long before their elimination, only barely managing to touch the legacy of their Mk. 2 counterparts. Still, though, they had a solid run. They settled well, defended well, and they created an identity for themselves. Plus, they founded a pretty major religion, which is never a bad thing for a civ’s legacy. The Bunuba did not win the game or even Australia, but I think they at least won a few hearts on their way to the grave. Better luck in round 3, Jandamarra. F.</p>"
