Though they may have exceeded many’s expectations, they still were eliminated in the latter half of the pack, falling short of achieving a Baltic unification that other competitors have flirted with in other seasons. Perhaps if they were able to achieve something against the Bjarmians, or hadn’t fumbled against the Hanseatic League, I wouldn’t be writing this eulogy now.</p><p></p><p>Estonia started out this game with a surprising amount of room, with only Scythia as their closest (and most dangerous) neighbour. Vyatka, the Bjarmians, Kalmar Union and the Hanseatic League all started a respectable amount of distance away, letting Estonia spread out over Eastern Europe. But border conflicts immediately began cropping up, as Scythia and Hansa both forward settled into their lands, and an early coalition war from Kalmar and the Bjarmians threatened their sovereignty right out of the gate. Though Estonia was plucky enough to fight them off right out of the gate, and even threaten the Kalmar city of Vassa, it was a poor portent of their long term survival in this game, with such aggressive neighbours already knocking at their door.</p><p></p><p>After that, Estonia continued to lock in. Spreading their settlers around the forward settlements that once threatened to close them in, Estonia made themselves a respectable empire, despite their encircled position. They continued to develop their cities, raising both Tallinn and Tartu to be tall jewels of the region. And, with a cheeky declaration of war against the Hanseatic League, Estonia decided that THEY would now go on the offensive. With both the city of Rostock and Bremen within striking distance, Estonia had a solid chance of making their empire the premiere Baltic power.</p><p></p><p>And then, they gave it all away. In one declaration of peace, Estonia gave both Hanseatic League cities back to the Hansa, and even tossed in an additional city in the heart of the Kalmar’s empire as an extra treat. It was a baffling move, and one that sullied Estonia’s plucky reputation for the rest of this game. How could such an underdog civ give away the clear advantages that they’d gained?</p><p></p><p>Once again, war befell Estonia as the Bjarmians and Kalmar, along with a bunch of the cylinders civs, declared their intentions of hate and disdain. Clearly their act of mercy against the Hanseatic League was seen as weakness, and these two civs tired and failed to penetrate deep into Estonian lands. If anything, Lennart Meri was capable enough to hold off invading forces, keeping a solid standing military to repeal invasion after invasion. And, enough diplomatic sense to keep Scythia pacified and looking the other way. Estonia even managed to threaten the Bjarmians capital, to limited success.</p><p></p><p>Estonia again and again became embroiled in war, constantly fighting off or fighting in the lands of the Bjarmians, Kalmar Union, or Hanseatic League. They threatened cities with their armies, but ultimately were unable to capture or hold them, locked in place by either swift counterpushes or lacking tactics. Estonia’s lands proved too difficult to fight out of, and with mainly flat grasslands and forests, with a smattering of tundra in the north, they were also unable to support a strong productive output. Their core remained tall, a pinnacle of science and arts, but unable to really pump out enough military to properly threaten their neighbours. With the Bjarmians to the north generally keeping pace with them technology wise, it was a constant back and forth as these two civs came to blows time and time again.</p><p></p><p>This back and forth with the Bjarmians would be their downfall, both wasting resources on wars that never ultimately made gains, and accruing negative diplomatic malus with their forever enemy to the north. With the Bjarmians being a religious leader, they were to hold more diplomatic sway over the region, and were able to mark Estonia as a problem civ to those around them. Though Estonia was able to flip multiple Bjarmian cities and threaten their capital multiple times, ultimately all Estonia gained was an enemy for life.</p><p></p><p>Estonia managed a tech leap in the midgame, beelining for naval superiority against the Bjarmians. Those Frigates and Musketmen proved very effective against their enemies in the short term, but ultimately led to no gains in the long run. This tech leap would be their height of superiority against their neighbours, and the beginning of the long term catch up that both the Bjarmians and Scythia embarked on. With a brief success capturing Rostock for the third time (and actually holding it for the first), Estonia found themselves face to face with their worst nightmare: an invasion from the Scythians.</p><p></p><p>Though technologically in parity, Scythia had what Estonia lacked: a swath of cities, a solid production base, and actual military tactics. Scythia’s first invasion of Estonia didn’t actually break that much ground, actually letting Estonia grab the former Hanseatic city of Bremen and the former Kalmar city of Bergen without a city loss of their own. But that one invasion opened the floodgates of coalition, and wore away at Estonia enough for the dominos to begin to fall. Kalmar then invaded, sending men and ships from the West to harass Estonia. Then the Bjarmians swooped in from the north, now with much superior aircraft that could bomb from the skies. As each of their neighbours continued to chip away, Estonia’s productive capabilities dwindled and their people grew more and more unhappy with their lot in life.</p><p></p><p>[Editor’s note: We can’t let Lennart Meri forget that he was the one who started the final Bjarmian war!]</p><p></p><p>The Bjarmians, those lifelong enemies of Estonia, proved too powerful with their access to air superiority. For the first time in forever, Estonian cities began flipping to their enemies, as riflemen marched through their lands. Their grasp on the Baltic continued to slip, as the wolves of Europe circled their prey. Soon enough, their capital was in the red, and then in the black, and then finally captured by plucky Bjarmian Cavalry. Though Estonia was able to retake their stronghold, the damage had been done and Estonia was left with a mere three cities, and a capital reduced to a mere three population from a grand 28. </p><p></p><p>The fall of Estonia was ultimately the rise of the Bjarmians, as they took the bulk of Estonia’s former empire. Despite exceeding all expectations, holding off invasion after invasion while also capturing and flipping cities of their own, this modern day state was unable to hold off the vast variety of more ancient, more aggressive neighbours. With Estonia holding on as a shell of their former selves for another three episodes, they were finally finished off by Scythia to their south, the most threatening neighbour that they had avoided for the longest time. As Scythian Landships rolled across the flat, defenseless terrain that plagued and benefitted Estonia throughout this whole game, Estonia’s legacy came to an end, a whole two technologies before either their Stadium or their Marine could finally be unlocked.</p><p></p><p>Estonia was ultimately a civ that was too focused on the non battle royale aspects of this game. Their position was good but surrounded by too many aggressive enemies, and their abilities were great but unlocked much too late to be useful in establishing themselves. Even their Unique Ability, that +5% great people generation per social policy branch, turned out to not affect Great Generals, something that is make or break in a game all about conquering your enemies. Despite things not going their way, Estonia and Lennart Meri made the best of a bad situation, providing us with an incredible underdog story against impressive odds. The ups and downs of Estonia were incredibly entertaining to watch, almost an orchestrated performance in a region that ultimately was pretty boring and underwhelming. Estonia was the best thing to happen to Europe this season, and them leaving this game is one of the greatest blows to X5 that we’ve experienced yet. </p>"
