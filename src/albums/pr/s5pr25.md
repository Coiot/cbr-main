  narration: "<p>NopeCopter:</p><p></p><p>What would it take for CBR fans across the globe to celebrate, from the bottom of their hearts, a bottom-10 civ placement? Not out of hatred, but out of joy? A top contender dying in 55th would be considered a grave disappointment. A weakling dying in 55th place would be considered expected, but still a bit of a letdown. Even a civ ranked 61st in the PRs dying in 55th place would be considered nothing too special (just look at Jamaica, who did exactly that). What would it take for 55th place to be considered an accomplishment not only worth acknowledging, but respecting?</p><p></p><p>Well, it would take a civ like the Xavante.<br><br>When the voting for CBRX5 began, nobody could have possibly anticipated what was in store for this poor little Amazonian civ. They had their pretty colors, their Great Musician spam, and a position that seemed plenty defensible. Perhaps they’d be the next Kayapo, or at least the next Kuikuro. Sure, they had a pretty scary neighbor in the infamous Guaycuru, but how bad could that be when they had the entire Amazon Rainforest to defend themselves?</p><p></p><p>And then the test images started rolling in. At first, it seemed like a fluke: how could a civilization - one in South America, no less - lose their capital by turn 40? But then it happened again. And then a third time. And a fourth. Something was horribly wrong. For some horrible, inexplicable reason, the Xavante had turned out to be an absolute invasion magnet. And it wasn’t just one civ picking on them, either - while the Guaycuru were the most common culprit, the Potiguara also made their fair share of early invasions across the test runs, and even the Yanomami got in on the fun on occasion. Heck, a lot of the time it was a group effort. Even when the Yanomami did manage to make it out of the early game, they were swiftly gobbled up within the first couple hundred turns, even despite their thick jungle cover. The Power Rankers were stunned. Plenty of times we’ve seen civs that just never do well in tests, because of a bad start or abysmal AI. This was something entirely different. This… was a curse.</p><p></p><p>But let’s say we don’t believe in curses here for a second. What was actually happening? Well, I believe it comes down to two key factors. The first is their neighbors. The Guaycuru were, of course, the obvious culprit. While they’re not quite the most infamously strong civ in existence, they’re still very well-known for their aggressive and expansive play, hence why they were ranked so high in the Episode 0 PRs. While the equally-strong Chono to the south muted their power somewhat, they were still pretty monstrous, and their early strength combined with pressure to head north put them into early conflict with the weakest and most accessible civ around. But there was another, more sinister second ingredient to the curse: the Potiguara. While they seem innocent enough at first, the Potiguara have a Unique Ability which grants them permanent vision over patches of Forest and Jungle adjacent to their cities - like, say, the Amazon Rainforest next to their capital. This meant that they could always see the Xavante from the very beginning, had a remarkably easy time expanding west into the Amazon, and most likely even considered that territory theirs to begin with… meaning they may have seen the Xavante as trespassers from the get-go. This cocktail of enemies made any move the Xavante took a wrong one, and likely gave their neighbors a hair-trigger temper that led to all their early invasions.</p><p><br>The second factor in the Xavante “curse” was just that they sucked. They did not want to settle cities even when they did make it out of the early game. I cannot stress this enough: I do not have a single screenshot of a test game where the Xavante have more than four cities. I feel like this was a bit overlooked at the time because of all the brutal Xavante murder going on, but man, they really did not want to play the game.</p><p></p><p>Now, normally when a civ does this bad this consistently, efforts are made to buff their start somewhat. For example, one-tile island civs might get an extra tile or two for their capital, or a desert civ might get some Plains tiles scattered about. But this couldn’t be done for the Xavante, because what was there that could be done? There wasn’t a way to make their neighbors not attack them. They couldn’t be given more food to work with, because they were already in one of the most fertile places on the map. They were unsalvageable. And so, the Power Rankers naturally placed them low on the Episode 0 Power Rankings. Really low. Sure, there were sleepy island civs like Ternate and Rapa Nui in the game who rarely achieved much of anything, but this was something entirely different. The Xavante were a civ that was actively hated by the world. The only reason they weren’t placed 61st is because some Power Rankers either weren’t up to speed with the curse or simply believed they’d perform in the final run much like Timor-Leste had. Even still, they found themselves in 58th place, expected to die horribly just like they did in every single run before.</p><p></p><p>When the game finally began, though, it seemed for a moment as if the curse had finally been broken, and we were indeed due for another miracle run. The Potiguara, for once, stayed away from the Amazon, and instead settled the coastline. The Guaycuru got off to an abysmal start. And perhaps most importantly, the Xavante had three entire cities down by the first episode, with a fourth on the way. The people were ecstatic. The Xavante rocketed up the rankings all the way to 45th, really only held down by the poor PRs still traumatized by how previous games had gone.</p><p></p><p>And then, like clockwork, the Guaycuru launched an invasion of the Xavante on turn 34. Less than ten turns later, the Potiguara joined in, too. Both civs had fairly top-of-the-line military scores, too. Fate couldn’t be avoided. However, it could at least be twisted, because it turned out that things would play out a bit differently this time. Neither the Guaycuru nor the Potiguara could actually figure out how to mount a proper invasion, and the positioning of the Xavante cities led both of their adversaries to split their already underwhelming forces. Before long, the Guaycuru invasion petered out, and the Potiguara got distracted and made peace after only five turns. The Xavante invasion had happened, but they had been ready, and they had defied fate.</p><p></p><p>Unfortunately, even this miraculous survival wasn’t exactly good news. The distraction caused by their neighbors had forced the Xavante to stop thinking about settling, allowing the Yanomami and Caral to expand into the Amazon and eat up valuable room for cities. That fourth Xavante Settler still hadn’t gotten a chance to do anything, while the Potiguara had started to absolutely pump out cities. Before they could even blink, even though they had finally built an actually reasonable number of Settlers, the Xavante had been crowded out. At the very least, though, they weren’t just a sitting duck. Their empire was small, but it was well-defended by the jungle, as well as a very healthy carpet. They’d missed their chance to truly defy fate, but they could at least continue the legacy of the Kuikuro and be a big annoying wall. They rose to 40th in the Power Rankings off the back of this military carpet, with some even daring to suggest that the Xavante could use it to expand despite their intimidating neighbors (and the Guaycuru). </p><p></p><p>The next few episodes saw the Xavante actually try just that, launching an invasion of the large but poorly-carpeted Yanomami. Unfortunately, though, the jungle terrain and hesitance on the part of the Xavante invasion force kept them from making any progress before they were stabbed in the back by the Potiguara. The war went nowhere, of course, because the Xavante had a full carpet and the fighting was taking place on South America, but it was a bit of a disappointment. The Xavante were stuck, but nobody could break them, either. Classic. It was around this time, though, that a rather concerning trend became noticeable: deforestation. The Xavante, unlike some of their predecessors, had no respect for Jungle yields, and had quickly taken to chopping down the Amazon that promised to protect them. They still had their carpet, of course, but this was very, very troubling for a supposed jungle turtle. The Power Rankers reacted accordingly, and this combination of stagnation and self-sabotage dropped their PR rank back toward the bottom half of the 40s.</p><p></p><p>The next few episodes were quiet for the Xavante as they focused on rebuilding their carpet for the next Potiguara invasion, which, naturally, didn’t take too long. This time, however, they were also faced with a new opponent: Caral. Caral was, by this time, known for their high Science output and expansion into the Pacific, but they were also less than unprepared for the Xavante war. In fact, they barely had any land units whatsoever! It was a perfect opportunity! Except, of course, that the Xavante sucked at war (and they were also distracted with the Potiguara, but naturally that war was going nowhere). The fighting didn’t really stall out so much as it never picked up to begin with. Even when the Guaycuru joined in against the Potiguara and started absolutely humiliating them, the Xavante were just absolutely stuck. And then, as if things couldn’t get any worse for their reputation, they committed the gravest sin of all: a completely unnecessary city gift. Intimidated by Caral’s (completely non-threatening) large and advanced military, the Xavante handed off the city of Wedeze, which was never in any danger, in exchange for peace. It was a devastating blunder, and worse yet, it didn’t even take a full episode for this choice to screw them. The Guaycuru, who had gotten quite ambitious after their underdog victory against the Potiguara, decided to follow it up with another invasion of the Xavante. This WOULD have actually been a slam dunk for the Xavante, though, because the Guaycuru had been utterly exhausted and their northern border was quite literally completely undefended… except that the Xavante no longer shared even half the border they used to with the Guaycuru, because they’d just given away the city that covered most of that border. Perhaps they would have just split their forces and the Guaycuru would have mustered up enough units to stop any gains anyways, but this was still a devastating blow to those who believed in this silly little underdog. Their PR rank plummeted as a result, knocking them all the way into a dismal 54th place.</p><p></p><p>The Xavante continued to flounder around in stalemate hell for a while longer after this, as is to be expected. They did kind of try to invade the Guaycuru, but not really. The Potiguara invaded again, but guess what? It was a stalemate. They had a fat military with empty neighbors on all sides, but it didn’t matter, because they were the Xavante. They couldn’t win a war! The Xavante front was a front that could be left unguarded. That was evidently the thought process of all of the Xavante’s neighbors… until they decided to try for revenge. Eventually, the Xavante got sick of being mocked for their ridiculous city gift, and decided to take another stab at Caral. Caral’s inland cities were as empty as ever, of course, but by this point the Xavante were falling behind on tech and still lacking in proper direction. However, they did have one key advantage this time around: they weren’t alone. The sleeping giant of the south, the Chono, woke up and joined the growing coalition against Caral. The damage was immediate. The Chono tore through Caral’s naval forces. The Xavante even managed to secure peace with Potiguara. Heads started turning. Then, the Xavante actually managed to start surrounding Wedeze. Then the city began to take damage. Caral could barely afford to spare a single unit for the city’s defense. And then, on slide 70, it happened: Wedeze flipped. The Xavante had actually managed to redeem themselves. But that wasn’t even the end of it. Having finally gotten a taste of victory, the Xavante decided they wanted more. They regrouped their forces and began marching on the Caral city of Huaricanga, Unfortunately, though, they got cold feet, and agreed to peace after knocking the city into the yellow. This was quite a disappointment, as they’d thrown away the opportunity for one of the most legendary underdog moments in the CBR, but most people were understanding. They’d retaken their city, and that was enough… or so we thought.</p><p></p><p>The next couple of episodes were another quiet period for the Xavante. Their age-old tormentors, the Potiguara and Guaycuru, were too busy fighting each other to look toward the (former) jungle, and the Potiguara even struck an open borders deal with their old bullying victim. It was calm, peaceful, and safe - exactly what the Xavante had wanted. But now, having gotten to experience the thrill of battle, they couldn’t get their minds off of it. They couldn’t stop wondering about what could have been. And so, they once again declared war on Caral - this time without the Chono to back them up. By now, Caral was far, far ahead of the Xavante on the tech tree, fielding Musketmen against Xavante Swordsmen. Sure, their cities were basically empty, but history has shown that tech always beats out numbers in the end. Without a second front to worry about, the inland Caral cities were reinforced in short order. But then… a miracle happened. The Xavante carpet started beating down on the Caral forces. And they started killing units. And this exposed a fatal flaw in the Caral game plan: despite all of their advanced technology, Caral had refused to build more than a few of their oh-so-powerful Musketmen. And so, as Huaricanga began to take damage, their defense force also dwindled to only a single Musketman surrounded by some ten Crossbowmen. Caral couldn’t be bothered to send reinforcements. The city fell into the yellow. Then it flipped. And then… the last Caral Musketman died. The Xavante had done it. They’d defeated Caral and taken a foreign city in a war, without any help.</p><p></p><p>Unfortunately, the rest of South America didn’t take kindly to this new upstart in the middle of their continent, and with the fall of Huaricanga, the Chono came roaring back to life to punish their former ally. However, here the Xavante were saved one last time by the terrain: not the jungle or rivers, as it had always been, but by the Andes Mountains they had finally reached. With their massive carpet and the lack of Chono reinforcements, some even wondered if they could repeat their shocking victory and take Aspero from what was by this point a top-three civ. At the very least, most figured that their natural defenses would keep them from losing any cities so long as planes didn’t come into play. If only they’d realized sooner the value of these natural defenses.</p><p></p><p>As the Xavante held off the Chono to the south and Caral to the west, another, more unfamiliar foe suddenly took the chance to act: the Yanomami. They’d spent most of the game as a fairly lazy power without much military buildup, hardly a serious threat, but by now they were teched up and ready to act. And most importantly, they had rap beef. The Xavante were caught totally off-guard. At first, it seemed like their city placements might save them once again, but before long it became clear that the Yanomami were actually moderately competent. The panhandle of Marimbu was easily crushed, and with nothing but flat land in the way, the Yanomami quickly moved toward the Xavante core. Even worse, the Yanomami actually had a competent unit composition to pair with their tech, combining Musketmen and Cannons to devastating effect. Caral and the Chono took the opportunity to break through the Andes and make a joint push, too, but they just weren’t fast enough to make any gains before the Yanomami blitzed through the Amazon basin. The Yanomami were brutally thorough, and within a couple of episodes, they’d taken every last Xavante city - although, as a bit of cold comfort, they wouldn’t have survived even with a peace deal. The Chono had unlocked planes by the time the killing blow was dealt.</p><p></p><p>So, in the end, the Xavante still failed to be anything more than a low-tier power. They were still the first South American civ eliminated, and they still died in the bottom 10. But that doesn’t tell the whole story, because if you compare them to what they could have been - what they should have been, even - then things really start to take a different spin. The Xavante lived for 25 episodes and over 360 turns. At their peak, they held 6 cities at once (their best performance ever). They not only did things beyond just rolling over and dying, but they even delivered MULTIPLE embarrassing defeats to a civ that, by all accounts, completely outclassed them. The Xavante did not do well, but they did get a chance to play the game. Their existence meant something beyond being a new record for the fastest elimination in CBR history. The Xavante mattered. And unlike some low-ranked civs with surprising runs, they didn’t just threaten to do something cool, but they actually followed through, too.</p><p></p><p>To use the words of a great scholar: for years, scientists have wondered, can you make grown men and women weep tears of joy for a civ that placed 55th? And the answer is yes, you can, as long as it is preceded by fifteen abysmal test game performances. Godspeed, Xavante, and may you have more polite neighbors wherever you go next. F.</p>"
